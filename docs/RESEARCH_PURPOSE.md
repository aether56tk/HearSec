# HearSec Research Purpose

## Final research identity

**HearSec — Evidence-Grounded, AI-Assisted Cybersecurity and Privacy Assessment for Connected Hearing-Aid Ecosystems**

HearSec is not intended to be a hearing-aid hacking tool or a generic Bluetooth vulnerability scanner.

The research purpose is to determine whether a standardized, evidence-grounded workflow can make cybersecurity and privacy assessment of connected hearing-aid ecosystems more consistent, reproducible, traceable and clinically interpretable.

## Why this is needed

Existing hearing-aid cybersecurity research has examined BLE information exposure, user/audiologist perceptions and specific technical security concerns. Broader Bluetooth, healthcare-IoT, medical-device and AI cybersecurity frameworks also exist.

HearSec addresses a narrower integration problem:

> Can evidence from the hearing aid, companion application, clinical software, cloud services, privacy documentation and authorized observations be organized into one reproducible assessment workflow, with AI assisting interpretation while human reviewers retain final authority?

This is a research gap statement, not a claim that no related framework exists.

## Research question

> **Can an evidence-grounded, AI-assisted framework provide a reliable, reproducible and clinically interpretable method for assessing cybersecurity and privacy risks across connected and AI-enabled hearing-aid ecosystems?**

## Objectives

1. Develop a hearing-aid-specific cybersecurity and privacy assessment framework.
2. Establish evidence provenance and assessment-status rules.
3. Validate the relevance and clarity of assessment domains with experts.
4. Apply the framework to authorized hearing-aid ecosystems.
5. Evaluate AI-assisted evidence extraction and classification.
6. Measure AI/expert agreement and unsupported-claim or hallucination rates.
7. Measure inter-rater reliability between independent HearSec assessors.
8. Produce clinically interpretable, evidence-limited reports.

## Study structure

### Study A — Framework validation
Experts review HearSec domains, criteria, evidence requirements and clinical interpretability.

### Study B — Authorized ecosystem assessment
Researchers apply the same HearSec protocol to owned, laboratory, demonstration or otherwise authorized devices and associated software.

### Study C — AI evidence validation
The same evidence is reviewed by human assessors and an AI assistant. Outputs are compared with expert consensus.

### Study D — Reliability
Independent assessors use the same evidence and protocol. Agreement is evaluated using appropriate statistics, such as Cohen's kappa for categorical decisions and ICC where appropriate for continuous measures.

## AI evaluation variables

- Evidence extraction accuracy
- Evidence classification accuracy
- Finding-status agreement
- Risk-classification agreement
- Evidence-grounding rate
- Unsupported-claim rate
- Hallucination rate
- Human-correction rate
- Assessment time

## Core principle

**No evidence → no invented conclusion.**

Unknown, unavailable or unverified information remains not_assessed or not_rated. An observation is not automatically a vulnerability.

## Safety and authorization

HearSec research must use written authorization and predefined scope for real-device testing. The project does not require authentication bypass, credential collection, unauthorized interception, persistence, exploitation or access to another person's data.

## Intended CV description

> Developed HearSec, an evidence-grounded AI-assisted cybersecurity and privacy assessment framework for connected hearing-aid ecosystems, integrating medical-device risk assessment, evidence provenance, human-in-the-loop AI validation, automated testing and clinical reporting.

## Important limitation

HearSec does not determine from symptoms alone whether a person's hearing aid has been hacked. A cybersecurity conclusion requires appropriate evidence and authorized assessment.
